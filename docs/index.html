What is this? iA plain-English intro for anyone landing here for the first time.

Sterling is a research project that asks one question honestly: can a computer actually pick stocks that beat the market — or is it just luck?

🎯 What it does

A machine-learning model ranks thousands of stocks each day and builds a “paper” portfolio of its favorites — no real money, purely to test the idea.

🧪 How it tests fairly

It trains on older data, then is graded on recent years it was never allowed to see — the only fair test. It also includes companies that later collapsed, so results aren’t flattered by survivors.

🛡️ Why you can trust it

It tries to disprove its own strategies. It will only ever flag something real — never a lucky fluke dressed up as a winner.

📊 What you’re seeing

Live results from the search: how many strategies were tried, how they scored on the fair test, and what the current paper portfolio holds.

How it works

The same disciplined method a professional quant desk uses — built to catch its own mistakes.

Survivorship-free data

~25 years of prices for thousands of US stocks, including ~38,000 that later delisted — so dead companies book their real losses.

Point-in-time features

Each day’s signals use only what was known that day. No peeking at the future.

Walk-forward training

The model learns on the past and predicts the future, rolling forward through time.

The honest test

Final grade is on a locked-away recent period the search was never allowed to tune on.

Tradeable only

Only real, liquid stocks you could actually buy — no penny-stock illusions.

Report the truth

A strategy is flagged only if it beats the market on unseen data with real confidence.
